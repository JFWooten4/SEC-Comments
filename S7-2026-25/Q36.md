# Question 36 — reuse of electronic addresses received from affiliates or third parties

_Source: SEC, [Electronic Delivery of Information Under the Federal Securities Laws, Release No. 33-11430; File No. S7-2026-25](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/proposed/2026/33-11430.pdf#page=65), Question 36 at printed pp. 65–66; see also the discussion and footnote 118 at [printed p. 63](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/proposed/2026/33-11430.pdf#page=63)._

## Draft argument

Question 36 should be answered yes. Regulation E-Delivery should permit a covered entity to use an electronic address received from an affiliate or third party when the address can be reliably matched to the same covered recipient and the third party is acting within the chain of shareholder recordkeeping, account administration, or regulatory delivery.

The proposal already recognizes that an email address supplied during onboarding ordinarily reflects an expectation that the recipient may receive communications from that covered entity about a variety of matters. Yet footnote 118 would generally prevent another covered entity from beginning e-delivery merely because it received the same address from an affiliate or third party. That distinction becomes unnecessarily formalistic where the third party is a common service provider, such as a transfer agent, that maintains the same shareholder's records across multiple issuers.

For example, an issuer may obtain a shareholder's email address and transmit that address to its transfer agent as part of the shareholder record. The same transfer agent may separately maintain an account or position for that same verified shareholder in another issuer. If the transfer agent can reliably determine that the records concern the same person, the rule should not require the shareholder to type the same email address a second time before the agent may use it to deliver covered information on behalf of the second issuer. Requiring repetitive collection adds friction, preserves avoidable paper delivery, and makes the legal result turn on which issuer happened to supply an already-known contact field first.

This can be structured without treating the email address as information that one issuer is entitled to obtain from another. A common transfer agent or other delivery provider can retain the address within its own system and use it to deliver covered information on behalf of Issuer B without disclosing the address, or any other information about the shareholder's relationship with Issuer A, to Issuer B. In that respect, permitting controlled reuse by a common agent can be more privacy-protective than requiring repeated bilateral transfers of the same contact information.

The Commission's stated concern—ensuring that recipients understand how an electronic address will be used and what covered information will be delivered—can be addressed by disclosure rather than by forcing duplicative data entry. On the first cross-issuer use, the agent should provide the clear and conspicuous disclosure required by proposed § 303.102(b), identify the covered entity on whose behalf it is delivering, explain that the address is already on file with the agent, describe the categories of covered information that may be sent, and provide a simple means to update the address or opt out. The rule should also permit a recipient to make a global election such as: “Use this email address for required shareholder communications from any issuer for which this transfer agent maintains my account or position.”

The permission should be limited to circumstances in which the address can reasonably be treated as shareholder contact information. It should not convert an address supplied solely for an unrelated purpose, such as technical support, into a regulatory-delivery address. Nor should it override the existing purpose limitation in 17 CFR 240.14a-16(k)(2) for an email address obtained solely to fulfill a request for a copy of proxy materials. Likewise, permission to reuse an address for covered information should not itself constitute permission to send marketing or other non-covered communications.

At minimum, a safe harbor for third-party reuse should require that the service provider reasonably verify that the records concern the same covered recipient; the address was not collected solely for an unrelated or legally restricted purpose; the recipient receives the required e-delivery disclosure before or with the first use for the additional covered entity; issuer-specific and global opt-out or update instructions remain available; the address is not sold or disclosed to another issuer merely because the provider is using it for delivery; and ordinary bounce-back and remediation requirements continue to apply.

A rule along these lines would preserve the proposal's notice and recipient-control objectives while recognizing how modern shareholder-service infrastructure actually works. The relevant question should be whether the recipient reasonably understands and can control the use of the address for covered information—not whether the same human being manually re-entered an identical email address once for every issuer whose securities the person owns.

## Possible rule concept

A covered recipient may be treated as having provided or accepted the use of an electronic address for covered information when a covered entity receives that address from an affiliate or third-party service provider that maintains an existing relationship with the same covered recipient, provided that: (1) the address was obtained in circumstances reasonably indicating that it may be used for shareholder, account, or regulatory communications and is not subject to a contrary purpose limitation; (2) the covered entity or its agent provides the disclosure required by § 303.102(b) before or at the first electronic delivery on behalf of the additional covered entity; and (3) the recipient retains the ability to update the address or opt out of electronic delivery.
